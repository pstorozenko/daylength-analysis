Warsaw day length analysis¶

Day length, sunrise/sunset times and sunrise/sunset directions for Warsaw over one astronomical year (March equinox 2024 → March equinox 2025).

All computations live in the tested daylength package (src/daylength); this notebook only orchestrates and plots. Conventions:

  • times are local (Europe/Warsaw, DST-aware);
  • sunrise/sunset use the standard almanac definition (upper limb on the horizon, 34′ refraction);
  • azimuths are degrees from North through East (90° = E, 270° = W).

Day length vs. a sinusoid¶

A least-squares yearly sinusoid (offset, amplitude and phase fitted).

1 harmonic : RMSE  7.1 min, max error 12.7 min
3 harmonics: RMSE  0.5 min, max error  1.0 min

The residual is not noise. Day length is roughly 24/π · arccos(−tan φ · tan δ), a non-linear but odd function of the solar declination δ, so the error is dominated by the third harmonic: summer days are flatter and winter days sharper than a sine. Adding the 2nd harmonic barely helps; adding the 3rd brings the error below one minute.

For comparison, the original approach (sine scaled by (max − min)/2 around the mean) had a max error of ~24 min, half of which came from centring on the mean instead of fitting the offset and phase.

How far along the horizon does the Sun travel?¶

azimuth_span = sunset azimuth − sunrise azimuth, i.e. how many degrees of horizon (through South) separate the sunrise and sunset points.

Halfway between the September equinox and the December solstice¶

The date where the azimuth span (and, separately, the day length) is exactly midway between its values on the two days. The search is restricted to the Sep→Dec window; a global search can return the matching February date instead.

Sunrise and sunset clock times¶

The shortest day is not the day of the earliest sunset nor of the latest sunrise (the equation of time shifts solar noon by several minutes around December).

Earliest sunset : 2024-12-12 15:23
Shortest day    : 2024-12-21
Latest sunrise  : 2024-12-30 07:45

Corrections compared with the first version of this notebook¶

  • Compass labels were mirrored. The polar plot used the default counter-clockwise direction but labelled 90° as West, so sunrises were drawn under the "West" label. Now clockwise with N/E/S/W.
  • Season dates were hard-coded and off by one day. 2024-06-21 and 2024-09-23 were used; the actual June solstice and September equinox were on 2024-06-20 and 2024-09-22.
  • "Halfway" target. It was (mean + min) / 2 over the whole year with a global search; it is now the midpoint of the values on the equinox and the solstice, searched only between them.
  • Sinusoid. It was a sine scaled by (max − min)/2 around the mean, with a 365-day period and the phase implied by the start date. It is now a least-squares fit (offset, amplitude, phase; 365.2422-day period).
  • Times were UTC and days were UTC days, starting at 01:00/02:00 Warsaw time; now local and DST-aware.
  • Day length used timedelta.seconds (silently drops whole days) instead of total_seconds().
  • Refraction depended on PyEphem's default pressure model; now the standard almanac convention, matching published Warsaw sunrise/sunset times to within a minute (see tests/).
